**Researched:** September 13, 2026, America/Los_Angeles.  
**Scope:** U.S. residential Xfinity Mobile, upgrading an existing phone line. Public offers, not an authenticated account quote. The existing phone, payoff balance, plan generation, line count, and usage were not supplied. No purchase or service change was made.

## Decision in brief

**Optimize the total cost of the phone and service, not the advertised discount or monthly installment.** The strongest candidate is the launch trade-in offer when an existing line qualifies without an expensive plan migration. The no-trade offer is useful when the required plan already makes sense; changing plans solely to obtain it can erase the savings.

There is no convincing historical reason to assume that waiting exactly one or two months produces a better existing-line deal. A good compromise is to obtain a written quote now and decide after general availability but before the advertised promotion deadline. Waiting for holiday promotions is more sensible when today's qualifying plan is unattractive than when today's offer already covers the desired phone.

Apple announced iPhone 18 Pro on September 9; preorders began September 12, with general availability September 18. Thus this is a preorder-period analysis. Apple's base 256GB Pro price is \$1,199; Pro Max starts at \$1,299.[^apple]

## What an existing customer can actually get

| Route | Verified public offer | Main qualification | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Launch trade-in | Up to \$1,300 off an eligible new iPhone; Pro Max advertised on us | Mobile Plus, eligible trade-in, new device payment plan | Best candidate when the actual quote covers the desired Pro and the plan premium is small |
| Launch without trade-in | \$400 off an eligible new iPhone | Mobile Plus and device payment plan | A genuine conditional discount, not merely financing; compare against keeping a cheaper plan |
| Upgrade anytime, formerly Elite Upgrade | Standard program advertises \$400–\$830 device credits; qualifying unpaid-phone returns can also receive balance forgiveness | Mobile Plus or Premium Unlimited, program eligibility | Separate from the maximum launch trade-in offer; do not assume benefits stack |
| Ordinary financing or leasing | Lower periodic payment without a separately identified subsidy | Product-specific agreement | Not a price discount by itself |

The first two offers explicitly include **upgrades on existing Mobile Plus lines**, not only new lines, and advertise an **October 8, 2026** end date. Xfinity Internet is required; taxes and fees are extra.[^deals] Comcast's launch announcement independently confirms the up-to-\$1,300 trade-in and \$400 no-trade alternatives for new and existing Mobile Plus customers.[^launch]

The headline maximum is not a personal trade-in quote. This research did not establish a complete current model/condition-to-credit matrix or the amount for a particular old phone. Do not assume a less expensive configuration produces cash back from an unused headline allowance. Extra storage must be priced separately.

**Do not import unrelated offers into the comparison.** The advertised \$15 Mobile Plus introductory rate is not the ordinary existing-line rate. The current plan page limits its first-year mobile promotion to qualifying new mobile customers.[^plan] Likewise, an additional line or a cellular watch is not free economically merely because its hardware is discounted. No targeted loyalty, referral, cash-back, or retention bonus is included in the calculations because eligibility and stacking were not verified.

An unlocked Apple purchase remains the useful baseline: Xfinity says Apple-purchased phones can be activated on an existing line.[^replace] The value received for selling or trading an owned phone is not all an extra subsidy; some of it is compensation for an asset being surrendered.

## The financing-term uncertainty matters

The indexed current Xfinity iPhone 18 Pro product listing describes **36-month, 0% APR** payments. Its JavaScript product page did not expose the complete personalized offer during this research. Meanwhile, Xfinity's generic device-credit FAQ still describes 24 months and new-line qualification, despite the specific launch advertisement explicitly allowing existing-line upgrades.[^product][^credits]

**Use the actual device agreement and promotion terms, not an old FAQ, for the final duration.** The main examples below model 36 months and show the 24-month sensitivity. They are not a claim that an authenticated checkout was completed. Apple's \$1,199 price is the illustrative device price; any Xfinity price difference, taxes, down payment, or rounding must be added from the cart.

Device-payment financing can be necessary to receive a real discount. That is different from financing at full price. Xfinity's credit FAQ says paying the device off, upgrading it, or canceling the line stops remaining device credits.[^credits] Consequently, a \$400 offer spread over 36 months earns about \$11.11 per month: after 12 months only about \$133.33 has been received. The remaining \$266.67 is not cash already saved.

## Measure the real discount

For a paid-off old phone and a full-term comparison against buying the same new phone independently:

```text
Net economic advantage of carrier offer
  = total credits actually earned
  - additional service cost across the entire account
  - net resale value of the old phone surrendered
  - extra fees and lost existing benefits
```

Count old-phone value once. For a no-trade comparison where the old phone is kept or sold in both alternatives, it cancels out. For an unpaid phone, separately reconcile its payoff, any existing credits being lost, and the balance actually forgiven; the simplified paid-off-phone formula is insufficient.

### A $400 discount can cost more than buying outright

Suppose the required plan increases the account's service bill by \$15 monthly:

| Assumed credit term | Added service cost | \$400 discount less added service |
|---|---:|---:|
| 24 months | \$360 | **\$40 saving** |
| 36 months | \$540 | **\$140 extra cost** |

These figures assign no separate value to premium data, insurance, or upgrade flexibility. Those benefits can justify the difference, but they should not be mislabeled as a cheaper phone.

For a 36-month agreement, the \$400 offer's break-even plan increase is **\$11.11 per month**. A \$5 increase leaves \$220 in savings; a \$10 increase leaves \$40; a \$20 increase creates a \$320 disadvantage. Use the total account increase, not just the price shown beside the upgraded line.

Using an illustrative \$1,199 phone, \$400 credits, and \$45 monthly service:

```text
Phone after all credits:           $799
Average phone payment / 36:      $22.19
Service + average phone:         $67.19 per month
36-month combined cost:       $2,419 before taxes and fees
```

Buying the same \$1,199 phone while retaining \$30 service costs \$2,279 over 36 months: \$140 less. This is why a low monthly phone price is not sufficient evidence of a bargain.

### Full trade-in coverage can still be worthwhile

Assume the quote covers the full illustrative \$1,199 price, the old paid-off phone could otherwise net \$300, and service rises \$15 for 36 months:

```text
$1,199 - $300 - $540 = $359 net economic advantage
```

The advantage is \$359, not \$1,199, but it is still positive. An unchanged service price would make the trade-in route much stronger. Conversely, sacrificing a valuable recent phone or a heavily discounted family plan can reverse the answer.

For trade-in versus the \$400 no-trade offer on the **same plan and term**, the rule is simpler:

```text
Choose trade-in only when:
quoted total trade-in credits > $400 + net value of keeping/selling old phone
```

For example, \$700 in trade-in credits is worse than \$400 plus selling the old phone for \$400. This assumes both routes are eligible and completed, with equivalent fees and no unpaid balance. Treat quoted promotional trade-in value as the total benefit unless the written terms explicitly add another credit.[^trade]

## What upgrading changes about data

Replacing a phone and changing a service plan are different decisions. The phone's 256GB storage is not a monthly cellular-data allowance. Buying more storage does not buy more hotspot data.

The current residential plans have these published features:[^pricing][^plan]

| Feature | Mobile Select | Mobile Plus |
|---|---|---|
| Regular service price with qualifying Internet discount | \$30 per line/month | \$45 per line/month |
| Domestic phone data | Unlimited; 50GB premium, then possible congestion-based deprioritization | Unlimited premium data |
| High-speed personal hotspot | 15GB, then reduced-speed unlimited hotspot | 50GB, then reduced-speed unlimited hotspot |
| Cellular video tier | Up to 720p | Up to 4K |
| International roaming | 215+ destinations; first 10GB high-speed per billing cycle | Same published high-speed allowance |
| Included device protection | Not included as standard | Xfinity Mobile Care Plus |
| Upgrade anytime | Not included | Included |

“Unlimited” does not mean unlimited high-speed tethering. Select's 50GB threshold is described as possible congestion-based slowing, not an automatic hard cutoff. Plus does not have that usage-based priority threshold, but radio conditions and congestion can still affect real-world speeds. Xfinity distinguishes WiFi performance from cellular performance in its network disclosures.[^disclosures]

For someone normally on WiFi and using modest cellular data, Plus's extra allowance may have little value. For frequent laptop tethering, the extra 35GB of high-speed hotspot can be materially useful. Both plans already include the advertised travel benefit; paying extra solely to obtain that benefit is not justified by this comparison. Review destination coverage and post-allowance speeds before travel rather than interpreting international “unlimited” as unrestricted high-speed service everywhere.

### Protect grandfathered pricing and other lines

Mobile Plus, Premium Unlimited, Unlimited Premium, and Unlimited Plus are not interchangeable plan names. The upgrade-anytime FAQ includes Premium Unlimited, while the current launch promotion expressly names Mobile Plus. Existing premium-plan membership alone is not proof of launch eligibility.[^anytime][^launch]

On the latest pricing generation, **By the Gig is unavailable**. Xfinity says migration requires existing By the Gig lines to become Select or Plus and prevents returning to By the Gig.[^pricing] That can be a larger economic change than the device discount.

The official migration flow presents choices by line, warns when a particular plan is needed to retain a promotion, and provides the estimated plan difference, next bill, and ongoing bill.[^migration] Inspect that complete comparison before confirming. Do not assume every line must buy Plus, nor assume other lines and discounts remain unchanged. Include any Internet bundle change in the cost model.

## Is the phone-upgrade program a good deal?

**It can include real discounts and conditional debt forgiveness; it is not merely a financing schedule. But it is not a guarantee of a free flagship every year.**

The standard published upgrade-anytime benefit allows a maximum of two upgrades per 12 months, at least 60 days apart, and advertises \$400–\$830 in device credits. Mobile Plus or Premium Unlimited and a new device payment plan are required.[^features] Do not interpret this standard range as either an extra credit on top of the launch promotion or a promise of its up-to-\$1,300 maximum.

There are three economically different paths:

| Current phone situation | What must be compared |
|---|---|
| Paid off and traded | Promotional credit versus the phone's resale value |
| Paid off and kept | No-trade discount; the old phone remains available to keep or sell |
| Unpaid and returned | Balance forgiven, new discount, credits lost on the old phone, and all continuing plan obligations |

Xfinity's dedicated FAQ describes the unpaid-return balance-forgiveness path and says the new discount is paid across the new agreement. It requires keeping the qualifying plan throughout that agreement. **Canceling or downgrading can both end new-phone discounts and make the old phone's applied payoff credit repayable.**[^anytime] An annual upgrader therefore cannot simply count a whole multi-year subsidy every year.

An illustrative trap: with a \$600 old balance, paying it off to obtain \$1,100 of new credits instead of taking \$400 plus balance forgiveness improves the full-term nominal result by only \$100, assuming both quotes really qualify. The extra \$700 in new credits cost \$600 upfront. Lost old credits and early departure could further alter that result. Obtain both written quotes before paying anything off.

The ordinary early-upgrade program is different: generally 50% of the current device paid, return the phone, and an Apple-device upgrade fee of \$30 without Xfinity Mobile Care. That is not the same as the premium plan's upgrade-anytime benefit.[^early]

### Protection has value, but not necessarily its advertised retail value

Mobile Plus includes Care Plus, not AppleCare+. It covers eligible devices and advertises unlimited claims, \$0 screen repairs and mechanical coverage, while service fees, deductibles, limits, and exclusions apply. Coverage is tied to active service; existing/BYO devices have different coverage-start rules from newly purchased Xfinity phones.[^protection]

For someone who would otherwise buy comparable protection, this may justify part of the plan premium. For someone who normally self-insures, it is a benefit rather than cash saved. Do not add a separate protection subscription without checking what is already included.

There is also a **condition-policy inconsistency**: upgrade marketing mentions damaged phones, but the dedicated upgrade FAQ calls for a functional, damage-free return.[^upgrade-marketing][^anytime] Do not assume a pre-existing damaged phone can be enrolled and immediately swapped for free; resolve the condition and coverage rules in writing.

## Launch versus waiting: what the history supports

| Historical snapshot | Evidence | What it does and does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| iPhone 16, holiday 2024 | A contemporary deal roundup listed a free 16 Pro with qualifying trade-in and a **new** Unlimited Plus line, expiring December 3 | A holiday headline can be strong but inapplicable to a simple existing-line upgrade |
| iPhone 17 launch, September 9, 2025 announcement | Comcast advertised Pro on us / up to \$1,100 with qualifying trade, and \$400 without trade, for new and existing qualifying premium customers | Launch itself was already a major discount window |
| October 30, 2025 | A customer forum post reported the then-current existing-upgrade headline at up to \$830 | Anecdotal evidence of an October dip, not a complete archived tariff or universal account outcome |
| Holiday 2025 | Comcast's syndicated release lists Pro on us, \$1,099.99 savings, and \$400 without trade for new and existing qualifying customers, through December 2 | Holiday offers returned to roughly launch headline levels, rather than clearly exceeding them |

Sources: contemporary 2024 coverage,[^holiday-2024] official 2025 launch,[^launch-2025] the October account report,[^october-2025] and Comcast's holiday release reproduced December 1, 2025.[^holiday-2025] The original corporate holiday release is dated November 18; its main body did not render during this research, so the readable syndicated copy supports the offer details.[^holiday-original]

This is a small historical sample, not a measured probability that future offers improve. Eligibility, trade-in tiers, service prices, and installment duration can change even when the dollar headline repeats. The 2024 new-line offer should not be counted as evidence that waiting helped every existing customer.

**Practical timing:** one month from this research date is October 13, after the currently advertised October 8 deadline. Two months is November 13, before Black Friday on November 27, 2026. There is no verified future offer in this research. Late September or early October offers time to evaluate the phone without deliberately giving up the present promotion window; inventory and continued eligibility are not guaranteed.

## Before committing

Get a written order summary covering three things:

1. **The phone transaction:** exact model/storage price, trade-in phone and condition, total credit, monthly credit, number of installments, any old balance forgiven versus paid, and remaining credits lost. Compare paid-off trade and unpaid-return quotes separately.
2. **The whole account:** each line's old/new service price, grandfathered plan loss, retained device promotions, Internet discount, taxes, upfront charges, and any unwanted watch/tablet/insurance add-ons. Check the ongoing bill, not just the first bill.
3. **The exit and return rules:** credit loss on payoff, upgrade, cancellation, or downgrade; any old-balance clawback; activation deadlines; and trade-in receipt deadline.

Xfinity's trade-in documentation ties the return window to the **new phone's shipment**, generally 21 days, and separately describes a 14-day new-device return period. Wrong model, undisclosed damage, or a late return can change or cancel the offer.[^trade] Photograph the old phone and identifier, preserve the written quote, back up and erase it appropriately, and retain tracking and acceptance evidence. Arrange receipt comfortably within the stated window rather than mailing on its final day.

**Bottom line:** prefer the launch trade-in when its verified net savings are compelling and the service commitment is acceptable. Prefer keeping a cheap existing plan over paying more merely to obtain a \$400 headline. Treat frequent upgrades as a separately priced convenience, and wait for holidays only with the understanding that the next deal may repeat, weaken, or change eligibility—not necessarily improve.
